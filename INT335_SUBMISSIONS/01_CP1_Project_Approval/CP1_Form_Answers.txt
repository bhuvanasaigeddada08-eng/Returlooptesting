[FIELD: Project ID]
P04

[FIELD: Project Title]
Lost on a 600-Acre Campus: Rethinking Lost and Found

[FIELD: Domain]
Campus Services / Digital Student Services

[FIELD: Problem Space (v1) — No Technology Named]
Students, faculty, and administrative staff who misplace vital belongings—such as student smart ID cards, laptops, calculators, vehicle keys, and wallets—across LPU's 600-acre campus face a high rate of permanent loss, extreme academic anxiety, and privacy risks. Simultaneously, honest finders and security personnel who discover items have no standardized, trustworthy, or low-effort channel to report or hand over recovered items, leading to unclaimed physical stockpiles at security posts and unmoderated social media posts where personal contact numbers are exposed.

[FIELD: Target User Group — Specific, not "all students"]
LPU hostellers and day scholars who carry high-value items across academic blocks (Blocks 32, 34, 36, Central Library, UniMall) and campus security guards/help-desk staff stationed at gate counters who accumulate unclaimed items.

[FIELD: Field Sites the Team Can Physically Reach]
1. Central Library Ground Floor Security Desk and 2nd Floor Reading Room
2. Block 34 (Computer Science Building) Ground Floor Foyer & Security Post
3. UniMall Food Court & Central Courtyard Seating Area

[FIELD: The 5 Mandatory Approval Questions]
1. Can you reach it?
YES — Our team members attend classes daily in Block 34, study in Central Library, and visit UniMall; we can physically observe and interview students and staff in person.

2. Who exactly?
YES — (1) Students who have lost or found items in the last 6 months, and (2) Campus security guards and library desk caretakers managing lost property boxes.

3. Problem, not solution?
YES — The problem describes the communication void, anxiety, false claims, and privacy exposure during item loss, without prescribing any software, website, or hardware.

4. Safe to research?
YES — No student ID numbers, passwords, room numbers, or financial details are collected. Verbal consent is obtained before all conversations, and interviews are conducted in the participant's preferred language (English, Hindi, Punjabi).

5. Can you prototype it?
YES — Low-fidelity paper wireframes, claim forms, and physical cardboard drop-point mockups can be drawn and role-played in a single afternoon sitting.
